The Multiplier

And Then There Were
Four Thousand More.

The six-person core did not find three lakh leads on its own. Over three years, LUDIFU ran a remote internship programme that more than 4,500 people passed through.

Running a distributed group at that scale is its own operational problem. Every cohort had to be onboarded, briefed, tracked and supported. That coordination ran through the same automation built for students — and through the same one person.

The Context

Built Inside a Lockdown.

In May 2020 the country shut down, classrooms emptied, and a generation of learners suddenly had time but nowhere to spend it.

The idea did not arrive as a business plan. The pandemic had stalled the family coffee table book printing firm — a business as old as the founder himself — and the photo album work alongside it. Looking for what to do next, founder Puneet Rawat found the answer inside his own house: his mother had been learning Excel from a tutor, and his wife suggested teaching Excel and PowerPoint online.

The first live workshops went out on 15 May 2020, mid-lockdown, priced between ₹200 and ₹1,799. Within three months there were over 400 students, most of them under 23.

We started LUDIFU Academy with a simple premise: online learning did not have to mean watching a pre-recorded video alone. Every session we ran was live and interactive — a real instructor, a real classroom, real questions answered in the moment.

Around that core we built the things that actually make learning stick: weekly doubt-solving sessions where students brought their stuck points, assignments that forced application rather than passive watching, and hands-on projects that gave every learner something real to show at the end.

Course fees ranged from ₹1,000 to ₹15,000 depending on depth and duration — deliberately positioned so a college student and a working professional could both find something that fit.

What We Took Away

Five Things That
Actually Held True.

01
Headcount is not capacity
Adding people to a repetitive process adds coordination cost, not output. The same six people handled ten times the volume once the repeatable steps were automated. The constraint was never how many hands we had.
02
Drop-off is not failure
A 2% conversion sounds alarming until you accept it as the baseline and build for it. Once we stopped trying to fix the percentage and started feeding the funnel properly, the absolute numbers moved.
03
Speed beats polish in follow-up
An instant automated reply consistently outperformed a thoughtful human reply sent four hours later. Attention has a short shelf life, especially when someone is browsing five options at once.
04
Live is the differentiator
Recorded content is a commodity. What people paid for was a real person, in real time, answering their specific question. Everything else could be automated precisely because the teaching could not be.
05
The demo is the product
More than half the funnel resolved before anyone saw a payment page. The free session was not marketing for the course — for most people, it was the entire basis on which they decided.
